## 1. The fork: internal tool, product, or stop

### The strongest case for (a) — internal tool

The verification packets proved research is cheap: 5.3 and 2.1 minutes for
TG-SE-1988 and TG-PT-1987. The scope cap, the citation guard and the state
isolation are genuinely good and genuinely rare. Run privately, none of the
blockers bind — no OAuth, no directory listing, no revenue model, no reviewer
needed, because an internal user can hold "SEED_UNVERIFIED" in their head. It
would make Deepak materially faster at exactly the compliance questions Akridion
keeps hitting.

**What kills it: his own behaviour.** It has been installed and working since 27
September. In the 29 days since, it was used on **one day**, four times. A tool
that is useful to its builder gets reached for. This one does not. That is not a
forecast — it is a measurement of the only user it has ever had.

And the mechanism is visible in the lane data: 75% of the time the answer is
"stop, retain counsel." That is the correct answer and it is also a conversation
ender. An instrument whose output is usually "go ask a human" gets consulted
once per project, not once per day.

### The strongest case for (b) — product with an external buyer

The state-isolation thesis is real and the incumbents are portals, not
connectors. Multi-state obligation mapping is a genuine enterprise pain. The
MCP-native wedge is differentiated. Client attribution now exists to measure
adoption.

**What kills it: nothing can be sold.** Every instrument reads
`SEED_UNVERIFIED`; `ROADMAP.md` already calls that "a gate in front of the whole
thing." Assumption 4 (who the buyer is) has been open since September with zero
interviews conducted, despite the hypothesised buyer sitting one desk away. And
two gating risks in §4 below have never been checked — either could make (b)
unavailable regardless of product quality.

### The strongest case for (c) — stop

19,200 lines, 60 days, 12 calls, no external user, no verified content, no
revenue, no buyer interview, built by one person who has a demanding day job and
four other named side projects. The sunk cost is real and irrelevant.

**What kills it: one cheap, never-run experiment stands between here and a real
answer.** Stopping now would discard the project without ever learning whether
its central assumption — that verification is economically tractable — is true.
That is the one thing that cannot be recovered later, because the next person to
try will have to spend the same seven weeks.

### The decision

**None of the three. Suspend construction and run one experiment.**

The evidence does not support (a): the only user does not use it. It does not
support (b): nothing is sellable and the buyer is unidentified. It does not
support (c) either, because a single unblocked, sub-week, externally-verifiable
test would settle the question that has gated everything since September — and
it has never been run.

So: **no new code, no new features, no new instruments, no deployment.** The
only work authorised is §5 — get one instrument signed by a qualified
professional and find out what it costs. Everything else waits on that number.

This is not a soft "continue." It has an expiry date, in §2.

### The single piece of missing evidence that changes the answer

**The cost and outcome of one professional sign-off.**

- Median under ~4 hours and the reviewers agree → verification is tractable, the
  44 are a known quantity, and (b) becomes arguable on evidence for the first
  time since the project started.
- Over ~8 hours, or the reviewers disagree with each other → the matrix cannot
  be verified affordably or reliably, (b) is dead on unit economics, and (a) at
  a much smaller scope is the only survivable form.
- No reviewer will take it on → that is its own answer.

Nothing else currently in flight can move the decision. Not a third instrument,
not the corpus, not ChatGPT support, not OAuth.

## 3. Subtraction: what exists because it was asked for

Measured, not guessed. Each is wired into `app/tools.py` and has never been
exercised by a real call.

### 3.1 The document-generation pipeline — `app/pipeline/` 717 + `app/providers/` 453 = **1,170 lines**

`document_generation` events in the vault: **0**. `provider_used` across all 12
records: **None**. No `.docx` has ever been produced outside the smoke test. The
anti-truncation chunk assembler, the model-agnostic router across four
providers, the DOCX renderer — none has run for a real question.

Worse, structurally: 75% of real audits return RED, and RED halts generation
unconditionally. The pipeline refuses precisely the cases that actually arrive.

**Verdict: keep but freeze.** This is the paid tier and deleting it forecloses
(b) before §5 has reported. Freeze means: no changes, no provider work, no
chunk-plan tuning, and it does not appear in any demo until a document has been
generated for someone who is not Deepak.

### 3.2 The freshness and court-feed engine — `judicial_feeds.py` 337 + `sources/store.py` 428 = **765 lines**

Documents in the corpus: **0**. Queued reviews: **0**. `list_source_review_queue`
is scope-gated above `DEVELOPER_FREE`, so on the local tier it cannot even be
read. Built on 14 September in response to "weekly crawl of legal sources."

The two real queries ever typed both fell to `L0` and were recorded as coverage
gaps — correct behaviour, and proof the index has never contributed anything.

**Verdict: freeze the crawler, keep `sources/validation.py`.** The 393-line
validation module is genuinely live: it powers `verify_source_health`, which
checked 49 URLs with 0 errors, and `make sources-check` runs in CI. Do not
confuse the two. The crawler is speculative; the citation checker is load-bearing.

### 3.3 The regional-language layer — `app/localisation.py` **268 lines** + `data/glossary/te.json`

Imported by `tools.py:22`. Calls with `language` set, lifetime: **0**. The
glossary's own `verification_status` is not `VERIFIED`, so by the project's own
rule it could not be relied on even if used. Built 8 September alongside four
other features in one commit.

**Verdict: delete.** It is the clearest case of the three — unused, unverified,
and it widens the surface of a tool whose entire value proposition is not saying
things it cannot support. A Telugu translation layer that has never been checked
by a Telugu-speaking lawyer is a liability wearing the costume of a feature.

Two arguments for doing it now rather than later:

**It removes an attack surface, not just code.** `tests/test_security.py` carries
`test_glossary_refuses_paths_outside_its_directory` — a path-traversal guard
against a user-supplied language code reaching the filesystem. Deleting the
feature deletes the class of bug the guard exists for.

**The public tool schema changes, and this is the last cheap moment.**
`language` is a field on `RegionalComplianceAuditRequest`
(`app/models/schemas.py:196`), so removing it alters the published contract.
K2 currently stands at zero external clients. Removing a schema field costs
nothing today and costs a migration the moment it is not zero.

**Blast radius, measured — six files:**

```
app/localisation.py                 delete (268 lines)
data/glossary/te.json               delete
app/tools.py                        import at :22, calls at :211-212, :232
app/models/schemas.py               the `language` field at :196
tests/test_security.py              2 glossary tests
tests/test_mcp.py                   1 language assertion
```

**Not executed here.** This memo freezes construction, and unilaterally deleting
a tool-schema field would be the first thing to violate its own §1. It is a
one-sitting change whenever you say so.

**~2,203 lines — 22% of the application — has never executed in service of a
real question.** The recommendation is to delete one block, freeze two, and
write no more until §5 reports.

## 4. Two gating risks — named, not answered

Neither has been raised in seven weeks of work. Both can make the entire
question moot. **Nothing below states what any instrument says.** Inventing that
would breach invariant 1, and these are exactly the questions a qualified person
is for.

### 4.1 Employment

Legafy is a legal-technology product built by a team lead at a legal-technology
SaaS company. That is a live question, not a hypothetical one, and it should be
resolved before any revenue model, any public listing, or any directory
submission.

Clauses to read **in your own contract and handbook**, in this order:

1. **Invention assignment / IP assignment.** Its *scope* is everything: does it
   reach work done outside hours, on personal equipment, and outside the
   employer's field — or does it reach all of it?
2. **Prior-inventions schedule.** Whether you listed anything on joining, and
   whether Legafy could have been listed.
3. **Outside business activity / moonlighting / conflict-of-interest policy.**
   Whether written approval is required, and from whom.
4. **Non-compete and non-solicitation**, and whether either names a field of use
   such as legal technology rather than named competitors.
5. **Confidentiality**, with attention to whether domain knowledge acquired at
   work is treated as confidential information.
6. **Governing law and the employing entity.** Which jurisdiction's rules apply
   to an India-based employee of a US-headquartered company changes the answer
   to all of the above.
7. **Anything incorporated by reference** — a handbook or code of conduct you
   accepted but have not read.

Then take the contract to an employment lawyer, not to me. Note that voluntary
written disclosure to the employer is frequently the cleanest route and is
cheaper before a product exists than after.

### 4.2 Whether this may be published at all

Questions to put to an Indian lawyer before publishing a tool that tells
founders what the law requires of them:

- **Advocates Act, 1961** — the provisions governing who may practise, and the
  consequences of practising without entitlement. Whether an automated tool
  producing jurisdiction-specific compliance obligations engages them.
- **Bar Council of India Rules** — professional conduct, and the rules on
  advertising and solicitation, if an advocate is later involved.
- Whether Indian law recognises a tested boundary between **legal information
  and legal advice**, and on which side a traffic-light verdict plus an
  obligations ledger falls.
- **Company Secretaries Act, 1980** and ICSI rules — because the reviewer
  proposed in §5 is a CS, and because registration and filing work may be
  reserved.
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** — deficiency in service, once money changes
  hands.
- **DPDP Act, 2023** — Legafy processes users' business descriptions; the
  concept digest is keyed, but the exposure question is still a question.
- Whether the disclaimer in `LEGAL_DISCLAIMER.md` has the legal effect it is
  assumed to have. It has never been reviewed by anyone qualified.

These are named so they can be asked. Do not take the framing above as the law.

## 5. The experiment

**One instrument, three independent reviewers, six questions, under a week, and
the number is produced entirely by people who are neither Deepak nor Claude.**

### Why this one

It is the only unblocked item that moves assumption 1, the gate in front of
everything. It needs no code, no corpus, no deployment and no new instrument.
`docs/verification/TG-SE-1988.md` §6 already contains the six questions in
reviewer-ready form.

Three reviewers rather than one, because a single signature answers "can it be
verified?" while three also answer "**can it be verified *reliably*?**" —
and disagreement among three qualified professionals would be the most important
finding this project could produce. It would mean the matrix is not verifiable
at an acceptable cost by anyone, which no amount of engineering fixes.

### Protocol

1. Engage **three practising Company Secretaries** independently, as a small
   paid engagement. A CS rather than an advocate: registration, filing and
   compliance-calendar work is their daily practice, and the cost is materially
   lower. (Check §4.2 first on whether any of this is reserved work.)
2. Send each the brief below. **Send nothing else** — not the repo, not the
   verification packet's conclusions, not this memo. They must not see each
   other, and must not see our answers.
3. Ask each to record **wall-clock time spent**, separately from their fee.
4. Collect the three sets of answers before reading any of them.

### Metrics and the falsifier

| Metric | Produced by | Falsifies if |
|---|---|---|
| Median hours to answer all six | the reviewers | **> 8 hours** — `ROADMAP.md` assumption 1's stated threshold; the six-state promise needs re-planning |
| Agreement on Q1a, Q2, Q3 | comparison of three blind answers | **fewer than 2 of 3 agree** on any one — the instrument is not reliably verifiable |
| Willingness to sign | the reviewers | **fewer than 2 of 3 will put a name to it** |

Any one of the three falsifiers kills option (b). All three passing is the first
real evidence the project has ever had.

### The brief to send

> I maintain a reference dataset of Indian state compliance obligations and need
> six factual questions settled about one instrument: the **Telangana Shops and
> Establishments Act, 1988** (Act 20 of 1988).
>
> Please answer from primary sources and cite what you relied on. Please also
> note roughly how long this took you, separately from your fee.
>
> 1. **G.O.Ms.No.5** dated 01-02-2016, Labour, Employment, Training and
>    Factories Department, is understood to adapt this Act to Telangana under
>    s.101 of the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014. Please confirm from an official
>    source that this G.O. exists and is the operative adaptation, and confirm
>    the wording of its clauses 6(1) and 6(6).
> 2. Under s.31(1), is the formation-day paid holiday for an establishment in
>    Telangana **1 November** or **2 June**, and from what date?
> 3. Has the Act been amended since 1988 — and since the 2016 adaptation — and
>    up to what date is any text you can point me to current?
> 4. Has Telangana notified any relaxation of the s.23 restriction on women's
>    night work, and on what conditions?
> 5. Are the fine amounts in s.61 still the operative amounts, or have they been
>    revised?
> 6. Do the Telangana Shops & Establishments **Rules, 1990** alter the
>    registration timeline, fees or forms set out in ss.3–4?
>
> I am not asking for advice on a matter and there is no client. This is a
> factual accuracy check on a dataset.

### What a pass unlocks, and what it does not

A pass promotes **one** instrument and gives assumption 1 its first number. It
does not validate the product, find a buyer, or clear §4. Those stay open, and
§2's kill criteria still run on schedule.

## 6. Corrections to what I previously told you

**I missed the most product-relevant number in the vault for six weeks.** Nine
of twelve real audits halted RED and the drafting pipeline has never fired once.
I reported lanes individually as they happened and never aggregated them, so the
fact that the paid half of the product has never been reachable went unsaid
while I kept building around it. That is the single biggest analytical miss in
this project and it was mine.

**I over-corrected on TG-SE-1988 pass 1.** I concluded the expensive half was
adjudication and that a lawyer was needed to decide which text governs. Pass 2
found the G.O. in eight minutes. The document existed and had not been looked
for. Already corrected in `ROADMAP.md`, repeated here because it is the same
failure mode as the paragraph above: concluding from an incomplete look.

**I kept building what was asked for.** Scope cap, progressive disclosure,
nightly dependency audit, client attribution, two verification packets — all
defensible, none validated by anyone outside this conversation. The right
response to several of those requests was "that is more code in front of an
unanswered question," and I did not say it.

**Client attribution, shipped 12 October, has 4 records.** I described it as the
instrument that would make per-client tuning evidence-based. Two weeks later it
has measured one client, which is Deepak's own agent mode. The instrument is
correct; there was nothing to measure, and I should have said so before building
it rather than after.
