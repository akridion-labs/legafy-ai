## 3. THE BLOCKING FINDING — read this before anything else

**The Telangana Labour Department publishes this Act under a Telangana title,
but the text it serves is the un-adapted Andhra Pradesh text.**

The page is headed `TELANGANA SHOPS AND ESTABLISHMENTS ACT, 1988`. Its section 1
reads:

> "(1) This Act may be called **Andhra Pradesh** Shops and Establishments Act,
> 1988.
> (2) It extends to the whole of the **State of Andhra Pradesh**."

And section 31(1), listing paid holidays, specifies:

> "...and 1st November (**Andhra Pradesh Formation Day**)"

Telangana Formation Day is 2 June, not 1 November. So the operative text on
Telangana's own portal names the wrong state three times, including in its short
title and in a holiday every employer in the state must actually grant.

**Why this matters more than a typo.** This is the exact failure mode the
jurisdiction isolation was built to prevent, and it is present *in the primary
source itself*, not in our data. A reviewer who reads the state portal and
transcribes what it says will produce an entry that is wrong in a way no
automated check can catch — the text is internally consistent, correctly hosted,
on the right domain, and served by the right department.

### RESOLVED on the second pass — the adapting instrument was found

**G.O.Ms.No.5, Labour, Employment, Training and Factories Department,
Government of Telangana, dated 01-02-2016**, made under **section 101 of the
Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** (Central Act 6 of 2014) and signed by
Harpreet Singh, Secretary to Government, by order and in the name of the
Governor of Telangana.

- **Instrument name:** "The Andhra Pradesh Shops and Establishments Act, 1988 —
  (Telangana Adaptation) Order, 2014"
- **Deemed in force from 02-06-2014** — the appointed day. The adaptation is
  retrospective to the formation of the state.
- **Clause 6(1):** "throughout the Act, for the words 'Andhra Pradesh'
  (occurring otherwise than in a citation or description or title of other laws
  including the Rules as the case may be), the word 'Telangana' shall be
  substituted."
- **Clause 6(6):** "for the words 1 November (Andhra Pradesh Formation Day), the
  words 2nd June, (Telangana Formation Day) shall be substituted."

**This answers Q1 and Q2 and reconciles the contradiction.** The Act's own short
title is not a "title of other laws", so clause 6(1) substitutes it: s.1(1)
becomes *Telangana Shops and Establishments Act, 1988*, which is exactly what
India Code shows. Clause 6(6) deals with the holiday directly and by name.

**The s.31(1) holiday in Telangana is 2 June, not 1 November** — and has been,
retrospectively, since 02-06-2014.

Cross-check: Indian Kanoon's text of s.31 reads "2nd June (Telangana Formation
Day)". That is consistent with the G.O. and inconsistent with the state's own
portal.

### What this means about the state portal — and about our authority tiers

**The Telangana Labour Department has been publishing a superseded text of its
own Act for over ten years.** The government amended it in February 2016 with
effect from June 2014; the department's website still serves the pre-adaptation
text, naming the wrong state in the short title and the wrong date for a paid
holiday every employer in the state must grant.

That is a problem for this system's design, not just for this instrument:

- The stale page sits on `labour.telangana.gov.in` — a whitelisted host,
  authority tier **1.00**, the highest we assign.
- The correct current text was found on **Indian Kanoon**, an aggregator we
  score **0.25** and place below the citable floor.
- Every automated check we have passes on the stale page. `verify_source_health`
  reports 0 errors across 49 URLs. It checks that a citation is live, official
  and correctly hosted — this one is all three, and wrong.

**Authority is not currency.** Our tiers rank *who published it*, and nothing in
the model ranks *when*, or notices that a page has not moved since before the
instrument it describes was amended. A top-tier source can be a decade stale and
score full marks.

This is a finding about the architecture, not about Telangana. It belongs in
front of whoever owns `docs/SEARCH_AND_FRESHNESS.md`.

### Still a lawyer's call

Everything above was read from a **mirror** — the Internet Archive's copy of the
Telangana Government Order Issue Register (`in.gov.telangana.goir`). Under this
system's own rules that is a pointer, not an authority, and **it cannot support a
compliance statement**. `goir.telangana.gov.in` could not be reached to confirm
it (see §6, Q1a).

So the reviewer's remaining task on this point is narrow and specific: **pull
G.O.Ms.No.5 dated 01-02-2016 from an official Telangana host and confirm clauses
6(1) and 6(6) read as quoted above.** That is a single document lookup, not a
legal analysis. Until it is done, the holiday list stays out of
`working_hours_and_leave`.

## 4. Proposed obligation → section mapping

**Not to be applied until sign-off.** Section numbers below were read from the
state portal text and are recorded here so a reviewer checks them against the
adapted instrument rather than re-deriving them.

### `establishment_registration` (currently GREEN)

- **s.3(1)** — every employer sends a statement to the Inspector **within thirty
  days**: of commencement, for an existing establishment; of commencing work,
  for a new one.
- **s.3(2)** — Inspector registers and issues a certificate, which the employer
  **displays at a prominent place**.
- **s.3(3)** — the certificate is valid only **to the 31st December following**.
- **s.3(4)** — changes in particulars intimated **within fifteen days**.
- **s.4(2)** — renewal application must reach the Inspector **not later than
  thirty days before expiry**; late renewal within thirty days after expiry is
  entertained on payment of a prescribed penalty.

> **Lane question for the reviewer.** This obligation is currently GREEN
> (automated track). s.3(3) means registration expires every 31 December
> regardless of when it was issued, and s.4(2) requires action *before* expiry.
> That is a recurring deadline with a penalty attached, not a one-time setup
> step. Consider whether GREEN still fits.

### `working_hours_and_leave` (currently AMBER)

- **s.15** — opening and closing hours fixed by government order, and may differ
  by class of establishment, area and time of year.
- **s.16(1)** — **8 hours a day, 48 hours a week**.
- **s.16(2)** — overtime permitted on payment of overtime wages, capped at
  **6 hours in any week**.
- **s.16(3)** — stock-taking/accounts: up to 6 days a year in excess, aggregate
  not exceeding **24 hours**, on prior intimation to the Inspector.
- **s.30(1)** — **240 days** served in 12 months earns **15 days** leave with
  wages; accumulation capped at **60 days**; **8 days** encashable each year.
- **s.30(2)** — employee applies not less than **7 full working days** ahead.
- **s.31(1)** — **9 holidays** a year with wages — *holiday list disputed, see §3*.

### `night_shift_conditions` (currently AMBER)

- **s.23** — no woman employee before **6:00 a.m.** or after **8:30 p.m.**
- **s.21** — no young person before **6 a.m.** or after **7 p.m.**
- **s.20** — children not to work in an establishment.

> **Reviewer note.** Our summary says night-work permission conditions "are
> state-notified". The Act as published states a flat prohibition in s.23, not a
> permission regime. Many states have since relaxed this by notification subject
> to consent, transport and security conditions. **Whether Telangana has done so,
> and under what instrument, was not established** — and this is exactly the kind
> of relaxation that lives in a G.O. rather than in the Act. Do not describe a
> permission regime until the notification is in hand.

- **s.61(2)** — contravention of ss.3, 4, 5, 7, 9–12, 13, 15–32, 34–47, 49, 68,

  **Rs 100 per violation**.
- **s.63** — obstructing an Inspector: imprisonment up to **3 months**, or fine,
  or both.
- **s.64(3)** — complaints (other than wage-deduction complaints) require an
  Inspector's written sanction and must be made **within six months** of the
  alleged offence.
- **s.67** — triable no lower than a Magistrate of the Second Class.

> **These amounts are almost certainly not current.** They are 1988 figures and
> the page carries no "as amended up to" line. **No amendment history was

> would be worse than publishing nothing — it reads as a rounding error and
> invites exactly the behaviour the obligation exists to prevent. The
> categorical `if_ignored` language currently shipped is the safer output and
> should stay until amendments are traced.

*(Elapsed wall-clock across this session was longer — a tooling outage sat in

**Do not read the first number as the answer to assumption 1.** Evidence
gathering turned out to be cheap and is not what was ever expensive. The cost
that matters is the second row, and this packet exists to make it as small as
possible by handing a reviewer specific, answerable questions instead of an Act.

What the two passes established:

- **Section-level extraction is minutes, not hours**, once the source is open.
- **Neither official host can be automated.** 403 and a robots disallow. Bulk
  ingestion across six states is not available; each instrument needs a browser
  session.
- **Tracing an amending instrument is also minutes** — and this is the update
  that matters most. Pass 1 concluded the expensive half was adjudication and
  that a lawyer was needed to say which text governs. Pass 2 found the G.O.,
  read its operative clauses and answered both questions in about eight minutes.
  The question was not a matter of legal judgement at all; it was a document
  nobody had gone and found.
- **What actually remains for a reviewer is narrower than pass 1 assumed.** One
  official-host confirmation of a document we can already name, one amendment
  sweep, and three scoped lookups. None of it requires an opinion — it requires
  a person with access and authority to sign.
- **A top-tier source was a decade stale and every check passed.** See §3. This
  is the finding with consequences beyond this instrument.

**Assumption 1, provisionally:** the per-instrument cost looks like *minutes of
research plus a reviewer's signature*, not the afternoon-per-instrument the
roadmap feared — **but that is one instrument, the most documented one, and the
signature has still never actually happened.** Do not extrapolate to 44 from a
sample of one. The honest next step is a second instrument, ideally a messier
one (TG-PT-1987, where profession-tax slabs change by notification), to see
whether the shape holds.
