## 4. Problems with our own entry

Two, both the same class of error found in TG-SE-1988 — our summary describing a
mechanism the instrument does not use.

**`pt_remittance` says the slabs "follow the state's own slab notification".**
They do not. They are in the **First Schedule to the Act**. A notification can
amend the Schedule, but the operative source is the Act, and a user told to go
looking for a notification will not find the rates. Rewrite.

**`applies_when` may be too narrow.** We list `employs_persons` and
`carries_on_profession_in_state`. Entry 40 sweeps in anyone with annual income
above ₹1,80,000, and the turnover entries catch contractors and dealers with no
employees at all. A single-founder company with no payroll can be liable. If
that reading is right, `any_entity` belongs in scope — and that is exactly the
kind of quiet liability a pre-counsel screen exists to surface.

**Possible missing obligation.** G.O. 169 inserted **s.5B for PT TDS** in 2015.
If that imposes a deduction-at-source duty on payments to professionals or
contractors, it is a third obligation we do not model at all, and it would
attach to companies that think profession tax is only a payroll matter.

### What the second instrument says about assumption 1

**The prediction was wrong, and the reason is the finding.** TG-PT-1987 was
picked because its rates move by notification and it should therefore have been
harder. It was substantially easier — and not because of anything about
profession tax. **The Commercial Taxes Department keeps a good website and the
Labour Department does not.**

| | TG-SE-1988 (Labour) | TG-PT-1987 (Commercial Taxes) |
|---|---|---|
| Official host fetchable | **no** — 403, and India Code robots-blocked | **yes**, every page |
| Current text published | **no** — superseded by ~10 years | **yes**, apparently |
| Adaptation G.O. findable on official host | **no** — only via an Archive mirror | **yes**, listed on site |
| Amendment history published | **no** | **yes** — 50 entries with dates |
| Research time | 5.3 min pass 1 (measured) + a second pass (unmeasured — a tooling outage sat inside it) | **2.1 min, one pass, measured** |

**The variable that predicts cost is not the instrument. It is the department
that publishes it.** Two instruments from the same state, both adapted under the
same section of the same Reorganisation Act, differ by roughly 2× in effort and
completely in confidence — because one department maintains its portal and the
other does not.

**Consequences for planning.** Per-state estimates are the wrong unit. A state
whose labour department is stale and whose tax department is current will have a
cheap half and an expensive half, and which is which cannot be known before
someone looks. The honest planning input is a **per-department source-quality
survey**, done once, before any estimate of the 44 is offered.

**Assumption 1 after two instruments:** research is **2–5 minutes** per
instrument where the publisher is good, and roughly double plus a second pass
where it is not. Cost is dominated by publisher quality, not legal complexity. The
reviewer's signature remains **unmeasured — it has still never happened once**,
and no estimate of the 44 should be made until it has.

**Do not treat the range above as the answer.** Both instruments are Telangana,
and both are labour/tax instruments with a single administering department. A
state with a fragmented publisher landscape, or an instrument administered by a
municipal body — Kerala's profession tax, levied through local self-government
institutions, is the known case — has not been sampled at all.
